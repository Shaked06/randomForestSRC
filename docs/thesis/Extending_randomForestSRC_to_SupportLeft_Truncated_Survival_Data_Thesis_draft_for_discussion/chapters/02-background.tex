\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\section{Observed data and risk sets}
Let $T_i$ denote the event time, $C_i$ the censoring time, $L_i$ the entry time,
and $X_i$ a vector of baseline covariates. Write
\[
 U_i=\min(T_i,C_i),\qquad \delta_i=\ind(T_i\leq C_i).
\]
The observed record is $(L_i,U_i,\delta_i,X_i)$ for individuals whose follow-up
extends to entry. The simulations described below generate censoring after
entry; their exact sampling sequence must be distinguished from a design
that draws an absolute censoring time before selecting individuals.

For ordinary right-censored data, the risk indicator is
\[
 Y_i^{\mathrm{RC}}(t)=\ind(t\leq U_i).
\]
The current package extension instead implements
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risk}
 Y_i^{\mathrm{LT}}(t)=\ind(L_i\leq t\leq U_i).
\end{equation}
Only individuals who have entered can contribute to the risk set.

Equation~\eqref{eq:risk} uses the inclusive convention specified in the
project outline \citep{projectoutline}. The implementation and LT scoring
helpers follow this convention, including left-limit conditioning at entry.
The Cox start--stop comparison uses a strict entry boundary, $L_i<t$.
Continuously generated times avoid exact ties that would distinguish the
conventions in these experiments; this does not establish equivalence for
discrete-time applications.

Estimating survival in a population from truncated observations requires
assumptions about the observation process and its support. Independence or
appropriate conditional independence assumptions for entry and event times,
as well as censoring assumptions, must be stated for each target of inference.
Altering a risk indicator by itself does not establish identification under
arbitrary informative truncation.

\section{Cox regression and survival forests}
Cox regression models the conditional hazard as
\[
 \lambda(t\mid X)=\lambda_0(t)\exp(\beta^\top X).
\]
Its proportional-hazards structure separates a baseline hazard from a
covariate-dependent relative risk. Delayed entry can be represented through
start--stop observations. In this study, Cox regression provides a comparator
under both correctly specified and misspecified settings.

A random survival forest aggregates survival trees fitted on resampled
observations. At each node, a subset of candidate variables is considered and
a survival splitting statistic selects a partition. Terminal-node estimates
are combined across trees to obtain predictions. The original random survival
forest method was developed for right-censored data \citep{ishwaran2008}.
For delayed entry, both the splitting calculation and the terminal-node
estimator must use entry-aware risk sets.
